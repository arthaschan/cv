Master's Thesis Research

Automatic Dental Multiple-Choice Question Answering via Knowledge Distillation

This research was not only about lifting a score. It was about building an AI research chain that could define the problem, compare methods, and validate results in a way that can be explained clearly.

Research question

I was not only asking whether the score could rise, but whether a smaller model could reliably inherit teacher capability

If distillation only wins once on a small test set, it is hard to trust in practice. The real question here was whether capability could transfer, which method actually fit the task, and whether the conclusion would survive at larger scale.

One-line judgment

The real value of this research is not one strong run. It is that problem framing, method choice, and result validation were connected into one complete path.

What that gave me was not only model familiarity, but the ability to define an AI problem clearly, work through it, and explain the result back in practical language.

Findings

These are the three judgments that remained after the experiments

Each point below is more than a score line. Each one corresponds to a method or experiment conclusion that can be defended.

Finding 1

Choice-Head is not a trick. It is a method choice pushed out by task structure.

Modules 02 / 13 / 15

It is better aligned with this task than heavier white-box methods and easier to explain and reuse in practical work.

Finding 2

Additional Stage 2 training does not automatically make a strong student better

Module 13

The 14B student reached 84.34% under single-stage distillation, while extra GT SFT could make it worse, which means method design must respect the student's own baseline.

Finding 3

A larger test set shows the student can stably outperform the teacher

Module 15

The 14B student reached an 88.67% three-seed mean and a best result of 89.10%, above the teacher's 87.18%, with only a 0.70pp range. That makes the result credible rather than just visually strong.
