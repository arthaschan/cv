Master's Thesis Research

Automatic Dental Multiple-Choice Question Answering via Knowledge Distillation

This research was not only about lifting a score. It was about building an AI research chain that could define the problem, compare methods, and validate results in a way that can be explained clearly.

Quick read

Start with what this research resolves

Can teacher-model capability be transferred into a lighter student model, which method fits this task best, and does the result still hold on a larger test set?

What matters most here

I did not only run a successful experiment. I carried the full path from problem framing to method comparison and result validation.

That matters because it turns AI work from vocabulary familiarity into task definition, experiment design, and result interpretation.

Findings

Three conclusions worth highlighting

Finding 1

Choice-Head is not a trick. It is a method shaped by task structure.

Modules 02 / 13 / 15

It is better aligned with this task than heavier white-box methods and easier to explain in practical delivery terms.

Finding 2

Additional Stage 2 training is not always better for stronger students

Module 13

The 14B student reached 84.34% under single-stage distillation, while extra GT SFT could make it worse, which means method design must respect the student's own baseline.

Finding 3

A larger test set shows the student can stably outperform the teacher

Module 15

The 14B student reached an 88.67% three-seed mean and a best result of 89.10%, above the teacher's 87.18%, with only a 0.70pp range.
