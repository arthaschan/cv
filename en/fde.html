Positioning

The hardest part of this role is not the model

Three other things are: the client cannot fully articulate what they want; the site is far harsher than any lab — no internet, constrained compute, data that is not allowed to leave the building; and the delivery date is fixed. Holding those three together is what I understand an FDE to be.

My position is simple: build AI into something that survives on site, and explain it in language the business can act on. If a use case will not work, I say so up front. If it will, I give a scope and a date that can be accepted.

Method

How I run an AI delivery

Five steps I have repeated on real sites. The order matters.

STEP 01

Frame

Get the client and the research team in one room and settle the single outcome that matters — and how it will be judged. This is what prevents endless rework later.

STEP 02

Scope

Break a vague brief into independently deployable modules, each with an owner, interfaces and acceptance criteria. This is the step that makes a project manageable at all.

STEP 03

Prototype

Get a thin end-to-end slice running on the client's real data within days, and take feedback before hardening anything. Never engineer on an unvalidated assumption.

STEP 04

Harden

Quantisation, offline packaging, monitoring, failure modes, capacity tuning — the work that decides whether the system survives on site.

STEP 05

Hand over

Acceptance evidence, audit documentation, training and handover, plus Q&A support for expert review. Delivery means the client can run it without me.
